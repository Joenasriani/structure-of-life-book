Method & source standards

This page defines what the study edition claims, how cross-domain transfers are labeled, and where the method must stop.

Publication status

The Structure of Life — The Structure of Reasoning is an independently published source-mapped study edition by J. Nasr. It is not represented as a peer-reviewed journal article, systematic review, clinical manual, or proof that people or biological systems are literally software.

The transfer problem

The book studies whether structural ideas used in software, engineering and systems thinking can improve reasoning about other domains. A useful resemblance is not automatically a shared mechanism. The edition therefore separates transfer strength instead of treating every analogy as evidence.

T3 — common formal framework

The same formal or mathematical structure genuinely applies.

T2 — structural correspondence

A relevant relation survives across domains without assuming identical mechanisms.

T1 — useful analogy

The comparison generates a question or model; it is not evidence of identity.

T0 — no warranted transfer

The resemblance does not support the proposed inference.

Observation and inference

The method keeps observed outputs separate from inferred structure. A label such as “unreliable,” “slow,” or “broken” can compress observations, assumptions and causal guesses into one representation. The reader is asked to reconstruct alternative models and identify evidence that could distinguish them.

Negative ontology

The edition records false equivalences and rejected transfers, not only successful correspondences. A structural vocabulary becomes useful only when its boundaries are explicit.

Source and claim status

Formal results, empirical findings, documented engineering practices, editorial syntheses, analogies and unresolved claims are not treated as interchangeable. Source records support specific claims; citation quantity is not used as a substitute for evidential fit.

AI Reasoning Framework

The included AI layer is a reasoning protocol derived from the book, not a claim that AI output is evidence. Its public core is: name the current representation; state omissions; generate competing structural frames; preserve evidence and transfer status; name discriminating evidence; then reframe or stop.

Limitations

The edition can contain classification errors, incomplete coverage, disputed interpretations and source-selection bias. Structural correspondences can be useful without proving causal equivalence. High-stakes scientific, medical, legal or safety decisions require domain-specific primary evidence and professional judgment.
